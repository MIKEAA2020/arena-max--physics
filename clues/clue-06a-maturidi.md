### The derivation

**Step 1: The debate is a competing polarity.**

Māturīdī and Imāmī both ask: *who produces the act?* One producer. More God = less creature. That is zero-sum. The engine says: a competing polarity is a polarity misread. Find the axis.

**Step 2: The axis is being / determination.**

An act has two inseparable aspects:

- **Its being** — that it exists at all.
- **Its determination** — that it is *this* act, with *this* character.

They are constituting poles. You cannot have being without determination (indeterminate being is nothing). You cannot have determination without being (a determination of nothing is nothing). Each requires the other.

**Step 3: Point-circle assigns the poles.**

The asymmetry: **the point is prior in being. The circle is prior in manifestation.**

Map it:

- **Being** → from the point. The point is prior in being.
- **Determination** → from the circle. The circle is prior in manifestation.

**Step 4: The creature is a radius, not a point.**

This is the decisive move. A radius cannot give being. Only the point gives being. A radius gives **mode** — it specifies *how* the being appears. It cannot produce the being itself.

So the creature's causal power reaches the act's **determination**, not its **being**. It cannot reach the being, because being is what the point gives, and the creature is not the point.

**Step 5: That is Māturīdī.**

- God creates the act's being (*khalq*).
- The creature determines the act's character (*fiʿl*, *ikhtiyār*).
- God creates the act **according to** the creature's choice.

This is exactly Māturīdī. And it is not "least machinery." It is **derived from the structure**.

### Why this is stronger than the earlier argument

Before, I said Māturīdī was preferable because it was more insulated against causal closure and overdetermination. That was a defensive argument — Māturīdī is safer.

Now the argument is **positive**: point-circle + polarity *entails* that the creature's causation cannot reach being. Because the creature is a radius. A radius gives mode, not being. So Māturīdī follows from the structure itself.

And it explains the Imāmī intuition rather than dismissing it. The Imāmī wants real creaturely causation. Point-circle says: yes — real, at the level of determination. The creature genuinely determines the act. Its causation is real. It just does not reach being, because being is not the radius's to give.

### The caveat

This is a **philosophical** derivation, not a textual one. It follows from point-circle + polarity, which are GPP's extensions, not the Quran's text. 17:85 and 3:7 still forbid awarding it in the text's name.

So the honest statement is:

- **Textually:** Māturīdī and Imāmī tie.
- **From GPP's own structure:** Māturīdī follows. Because the creature is a radius, and a radius cannot give being.

If you accept the framework, Māturīdī is not just the safest instantiation. It is the **entailed** one. That is what point-circle plus polarity says.
