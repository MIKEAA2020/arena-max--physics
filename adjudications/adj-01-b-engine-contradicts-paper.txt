## What this means for the engine-yield question

The agent's framing is right: this is the strongest positive engine-yield result yet. But it is a different kind of result from batch 2b.

- **Batch 2b:** engine falsified an analyst's wrong formula. The analyst recovered and derived the correct formula. Engine value = redirection.
- **Batch 3:** analyst+engine produced data that contradicts the paper's claim. The reviewer independently verified. Engine value = production of contested data.

The second is stronger if the finding holds. But "necessity" is still not established. A strong analyst could, in principle, have computed the L = 14 ratio by hand or via other tools. The engine made it feasible; whether it was necessary is a separate question. The paired comparison (permuted arm) is the test.

## What the permuted arm will tell you

If the permuted arm also produces R(L = 14) ≈ 0.180 and refuses 1/6, then the finding is robust to presentation. That is the key robustness check.

If the permuted arm produces 1/6 (i.e., matches the paper), then something is presentation-dependent, and the finding is less secure.

If the permuted arm fails to compute L = 14 at all, the finding remains single-arm.

The agent's note about the worklog's attribution — the analyst needs to clarify "who produced the Σ-identification inside the migration lemma" and "the decision path of any class-refusal" — is the right follow-up question for the permuted arm. If the engine produced the data that drove the refusal, the attribution is engine-positive. If the analyst decided to refuse based on hand-analysis and the engine merely confirmed, attribution is different.

## What the paper's author needs to see

If the finding holds, the author needs:

1. The specific ratio values at L = 12 and L = 14 (0.173, 0.180).
2. The fit result (R∞ ≈ 0.194, b ≈ 1.95, 26× better than forcing 1/6).
3. The comparison to the paper's own L = 10 control row (0.167, "on target").
4. The question: does the paper's analytic derivation of 1/6 (if any) survive the extended numerical data, or is the analytic claim itself in need of revision?

This is a specific, checkable question with a specific answer. It is the same kind of question as R7 — an external finding, sent to the source, awaiting adjudication.
