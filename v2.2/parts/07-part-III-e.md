## §16 Measurement, actualization, and the Born rule

The problem of time and the measurement problem meet at one point: quantum mechanics needs a *now* at which the state is defined and probabilities normalized (§7.2), and whatever delivers definite outcomes delivers them *in* time. This section states the Lighthouse's commitments on measurement in their strongest logical form, prices them, and records what the price buys for the time problem.

**16.1 The commitments, in their strongest form.** The Lighthouse holds (v1.0 §4): **D-7**, no collapse — there is no physical process that replaces the unitary dynamics; **D-10**, the Born rule is recovered from the measure structure, not postulated; and the definite outcome is an **I2**-intrinsic fact, absolute rather than perspectival (§8). Logically there are four ways to have outcomes:

1. *Many outcomes* (Everett). Excluded by the I2 commitment: there is one actual outcome. (The Lighthouse's reason is first-principles — facts are known to God singly — and it is entered as a premise, not a result.)
2. *Dynamical collapse* (GRW, CSL, Diósi–Penrose). D-7 bets against the whole family. Status of the bet: live and tightening. The parameter-free Diósi–Penrose model is excluded by the Gran Sasso search for collapse-induced radiation (Donadi et al. 2021); the Majorana Demonstrator (Arnquist et al. 2022) further constrains CSL and Diósi–Penrose parameter space; the XENONnT search for collapse-induced X-rays (1–140 keV, first science run) sets the current world-leading limits — CSL tightened by two orders of magnitude, Diósi–Penrose by a factor of five (XENONnT Collaboration, PRL 136 (2026) 120201); interferometry with large molecules and levitated-nanoparticle proposals press from the other side. *Falsifier of D-7:* a positive detection of a collapse signature — anomalous heating, spontaneous radiation, or interferometric visibility loss beyond all decoherence accounting. This is the testable content the audit asked D-7 to have.
3. *Relative facts* (relational quantum mechanics; QBism). Definiteness relative to a system or agent. **Overridden** (§8): facts are absolute.
4. *Single outcome without collapse*: the state plus something determines the actual value. This is the Lighthouse's position, and it is, by definition, a **hidden-variable theory** in Bell's sense — whether the "something" is a configuration (Bohm), a stochastic process (Nelson), or a measure over actual values (the Lighthouse's own M2 phrasing). (What this position does to *time* is now stated: the pilot-wave row of §7.3 and the trajectory-time row of §9.4 — the trajectory supplies order and duration; the price is a distinguished foliation; the arrow is still not supplied.)

**16.2 The price of position 4 — theorems.**

| Theorem | What it forces on position 4 |
|---|---|
| Bell 1964 (established experimentally; loophole-free 2015) | Nonlocal dependence of outcomes on distant settings |
| Kochen–Specker 1967; Spekkens 2005 | Contextuality: the actual value depends on which compatible observables are co-measured |
| Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph 2012 | Under preparation independence the quantum state is ontic — not merely knowledge of the hidden variables |
| Frauchiger–Renner 2018; Bong et al. 2020 ("local friendliness") | *Absoluteness of observed events* + locality + no-superdeterminism is inconsistent with quantum predictions (and the inequality is violated experimentally in proof-of-principle form — photonic friends in Bong et al. 2020; friends of growing "branch factor" and, in 2026, agent-like friends with memory and conditional action on superconducting processors, loopholes open: Zeng–Labib–Russo, Quantum 9 (2025) 1851; Laux–Cavalcanti, arXiv:2609.12527). With absoluteness held (§8), **actualization is nonlocal**, not merely correlation |
| No-signalling (theorem of the formalism) | None of the above is usable for signalling; the nonlocality is at the level of facts, not of messages |

**G20** is the ledger entry: the Lighthouse's measurement position is a nonlocal, contextual, ψ-ontic hidden-variable account with absolute outcomes. The Lighthouse can pay, because it already holds locality to be emergent (v1.0 §3) and it has no particle ontology (§13.2) — but it must be seen to pay, and no sentence of this document may describe the position as "just quantum mechanics without collapse."

**The I2 trade-off (admitted to the inventory, §4).** The pair *intrinsic (I2) ↔ relational* passes the trade-off test in this form: the more an outcome is treated as context-independent (an I2 fact fixed before the context), the more nonlocality and contextuality the account must carry, and the amount is quantified — by the Bell and Kochen–Specker inequality violations, and more finely by the contextual fraction of Abramsky–Brandenburger 2011, which bounds the share of any empirical model that admits a context-independent explanation. That is an inequality, which is what band 1 of the map requires.

**16.3 D-10: Born from measure.** *Theorem:* on a Hilbert space of dimension ≥ 3, any countably additive probability assignment on projections is given by a density operator (Gleason 1957); for effects (POVMs) the result holds in all dimensions ≥ 2 (Busch 2003; Caves–Fuchs–Manne–Renes 2004). So the Born rule is the *only* noncontextual probability measure on the quantum event structure. What D-10 adds, and what remains conjectural: that the measure in Gleason's theorem is the state of the world in sense M2; that it is the *same* measure whose flow is time (Conjecture M, §12.2); and — for position 4 — that the actual values are distributed according to it. The last has one worked instance: in Bohmian mechanics the Born distribution is derived as *typicality* with respect to the natural measure on configurations (Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì 1992) — "Born from measure" in exactly the sense D-10 means, with the equilibrium hypothesis as its contingency. Alternative derivations (Deutsch–Wallace decision theory; Zurek's envariance) are recorded with their contested assumptions and are not relied on. A4/G12 — the conditioning-to-measure map in the *relational* setting, where the "now" is a clock reading — is the open atom. *Tenth amendment (§22.13):* the typicality step has its algebraic foundation — the Lubkin–Page bounds hold for all type II factors, including the clock-dressed gravitational algebras, and fail for type III (Wang–Braunstein, PRD 113 (2026) L121904); what remains open is unchanged, the relational conditioning map A4.

**16.4 What the plan may and may not say about actualization.** It may say: outcomes are I2 facts; the measure is the Born measure; the mechanism is unknown; the account is nonlocal. It may not say: that God actualizes outcomes (§6, item 1 — the ground upholds everything and is a mechanism of nothing, rule 7 of §2); that consciousness actualizes outcomes (item 8); that "field collapse" is unproblematic (§13.2); that relational definiteness is enough (§8). The v2.0 glue "relational actualization = the collapse reading" (G14) is kept only as its *physics* component: the conditioning-to-record map through decoherence (WP7).

**In plain terms.** The Lighthouse says: one outcome really happens, nothing in nature "collapses" to make it happen, and the odds are the ones the quantum state already carries. Put that way, it is a hidden-variable view, and the theorems of the last sixty years say what such a view costs: the fact of an outcome here depends on choices made far away, depends on what else was measured alongside, and the quantum state is a real thing, not an opinion. The Lighthouse can afford this — it never believed locality was fundamental — but it must say the price out loud. The one thing it gets for free is the probability rule: mathematics shows there is only one consistent way to assign odds to quantum events, and the bet is that this one measure is also the measure whose flow is time.

## §17 The arrow of time

**17.1 Two problems, not one.** The source documents' "arrow specification" ran together two questions that must be separated (audit of clue #2):

- **(a) The low-entropy condition.** Why was the entropy of the early universe so low (Penrose's Weyl-curvature hypothesis; Albert's Past Hypothesis)? Given time-symmetric laws, this condition, not the laws, produces the thermodynamic arrow, and with it the arrows of records, memory, causation and radiation (strong consensus). *Status of the secondary arrows, made precise (15 October 2026, ninth amendment; clue 12):* the **records and memory arrows are derivative at theorem grade** — a trace of the past exists only given an earlier lower-entropy state (Reichenbach 1956; Albert 2000), the thermodynamic conditions sufficient for macroscopic traces are explicit (Rovelli 2022: system separation, temperature differences, long thermalization times — "low entropy transformed into available information"), and *any* physical system that preserves a record, dissipative or not, must have its arrow aligned with the thermodynamic arrow, from robustness of that arrow plus the requirement that a memory not be fine-tuned to what it records (Mlodinow–Brun 2014); the division into past, present and future is a description of how information-gathering-and-utilizing systems process information, consistent with four-dimensional physics (Hartle 2005); and when the universe's entropy dynamics is formalized as a time-symmetric, time-translation-invariant Markov process, every asymmetry of memory, every Boltzmann-brain argument and the second law itself turn out to depend on a *conditioning time* that the process does not supply (Wolpert–Rovelli–Scharnhorst, Entropy 27 (2025) 1227; tenth amendment) — Proposition 11.1's pattern in classical dress. It follows that an argument from the brain's temporal asymmetry — memory of the past, Bayesian prediction of the future, "no encoding mechanism for future events" — to the *illusoriness* of the thermodynamic arrow runs backwards: the asymmetry it cites is an instance of the arrow, not its source (same error family as §23.2 items 78 (D4) and 90). What remains partial is the derivation of the **radiative** and **causal** arrows from the thermodynamic one.
- **(b) The orientation.** Why is there a single, global direction at all — rather than two arrows pointing away from a middle (Gold's two-boundary universe; Barbour–Koslowski–Mercati's Janus point in the N-body problem, 2014; Carroll–Chen's baby universes nucleating in both directions from a high-entropy middle, 2004)? Time-symmetric laws plus one boundary condition yield an orientation only relative to that boundary; nothing in (a) makes the orientation unique. Orientation is not even locally forced: regions with opposite thermodynamic arrows can coexist and interact weakly (Schulman, PRL 83 (1999) 5419). *The live alternative to D-8's reading of (a) (tenth amendment; §22.13):* in the Janus-point line's strongest form, the global spatial scale is treated as surplus structure, the scale-reduced dynamics has universal attractors and Janus points, and an arrow is claimed without time-asymmetric laws *and without a Past Hypothesis* — generic rather than lawful-contingent (Gryb–Friederich 2025, preprint; numerical support for the growth of structural complexity away from the Janus point in Barbour–Lobo–Lourenço 2026, preprint). The plan's position: a generic arrow is still an arrow relative to the Janus point, two-sided; what makes our side ours is a condition on where we are, not on the laws — (a) relocated, not removed. If the relocation is complete, D-8's demand on (a) is met trivially and the plan must say so; registered as the alternative, not adopted.

Under the Lighthouse, (a) is a case of **D-8**: the selection is contingent (which is expected of a created order) and must be *lawful* — derived from a principle, not posited as a brute fact. (b) is split by register. *(Independent support, 15 October 2026 — §11.9, §23.2 item 66: the clue-6b manuscript proves for its arithmetic clock that an almost-periodic correlation kernel carries no arrow, relocates every arrow to interaction plus boundary condition, and demotes its own "low-entropy past as arithmetic necessity" to a conjecture about one distinguished state — which is (a) stated from the other side.)*

*Candidates and falsifiers for (a), carried from the arrow specification (clue 2) with their scope restored (§23.2, item 58).* The specification named three existing proposals for a lawful low-entropy condition, each of which makes CMB-level predictions: the **Weyl curvature hypothesis** (Penrose 1979 — vanishing Weyl curvature at the initial singularity; a specific low-entropy state), the **no-boundary proposal** (Hartle–Hawking 1983 — the initial state selected by a Euclidean path integral; a specific primordial power spectrum, no initial singularity) and the **tunnelling proposal** (Vilenkin 1982 — a different spectrum); a fourth form, stated in entanglement rather than thermodynamic entropy, is the *entanglement past hypothesis* (Al-Khalili–Chen, Found. Phys. 54 (2024); tenth amendment). *Epistemic ceiling on all four (tenth amendment):* typical state vectors in the low-entropy subspace are observationally indistinguishable — no observation identifies, or appreciably narrows, the universal state within it (Chen–Tumulka 2024, preprint) — so D-8, as applied to (a), is a claim about the lawfulness of the *subspace*, not of the vector (G19). Under Conjecture M these are candidate *forms* of A9: a derivation of the low-entropy condition from the measure structure, not a stipulated boundary condition. The specification's falsifiers stand, re-scoped: (i) a final theory in which the initial state is a landscape of environmental conditions falsifies D-8 as applied to (a) — one bet with two consequences; (ii) exclusion of both wavefunction proposals by the CMB with no derivable alternative in sight puts the bet in trouble, not out; (iii) a two-ended, Carroll–Chen-type universe — low entropy at both ends, reversing in the middle — would kill the *orientation* claim, which this plan has already withdrawn from physics (b), and would leave (a) untouched. What the bet still does not give is a number: not which state, not the tensor-to-scalar ratio; it gives the form of the answer — derivable, not arbitrary — and a derivation is the target. In the first-principles register the created order has one direction; that is a revelation claim and may not be entered as physics (§6, items 2, 10, 14). In the physics register (b) is **open**: the Janus-point models show that a single orientation is not forced by the laws, and no result of this plan favours one orientation over two. The plan predicts nothing about (b).

**17.2 The modular correction.** v2.0 proposed that the arrow be the positivity of a modular Hamiltonian in a "modular ground state." Proposition 11.1 shows there is no such state on any type III algebra, and that in the clock-dressed type II₁ algebra the one distinguished state is the trace — *maximum* entropy, the arrow's far end. The arrow therefore enters the modular programme in two pieces: the **generalized second law in gravitational algebras** (Faulkner–Speranza 2024; a local form in crossed products for static and Kerr black holes, Ali–Suneeta 2024, preprint), which is the arrow *in form* — entropy relative to the trace does not decrease along the flow — and the **low-entropy condition relative to the trace**, which is the arrow *in fact* and is contingent. The second is atom A9. The process-theoretic side says the same (§10.7): exact reversal is prior-indexed — the Petz transpose is a functor on *pointed* channels, built from the same relative modular data as the flow — so the arrow is carried by the state, never by the category of processes or by the algebra alone. The plan's bet, which is the Lighthouse's D-8 applied here: A9 is lawful — there is a principle (a measure-first principle, if Conjecture M holds: the initial state is distinguished by the same M2/M3 structure that fixes the flow) from which the condition follows. *Falsifier:* a demonstration that the low-entropy condition is a free parameter of the final theory with no derivation — the brute-fact outcome.

**17.3 The cosmological measure problem.** In eternal-inflation cosmology the probabilities of observations depend on a measure over an infinite ensemble of pocket universes, and no measure is agreed (the Boltzmann-brain and youngness paradoxes are symptoms). This is an M2 problem, and it is the arrow problem's cosmological form. **D-8** makes a sharp prediction here: *there is no fundamental landscape*, hence no measure over pocket universes to choose; the measure problem is predicted to disappear with the landscape, not to be solved within it. If the landscape is real and the measure problem genuine, D-8 fails and with it a central Lighthouse bet (§18). The plan does not hedge this.

**17.3a Laboratory analogues, scoped** *(restored from v2.0 §8.2; dropped in v2.1 — §23.2, item 61).* Two verified results give the intrinsic/extrinsic split and the arrow a laboratory form, and each contributes exactly one scoped observation. In **time-varying media** (Koivurova, Robson and Ornigotti 2023) a wave whose propagation speed changes in time obeys an accelerating wave equation admitting only forward solutions, with time reversal possible only at zero acceleration; the wave carries an intrinsic time distinct from laboratory time, and the acceleration induces a non-stationary effective metric — the sphere analogy of §10.1 realized in classical wave mechanics. Its "microscopic arrow" is a model-specific asymmetry, not a derivation of the arrow. In **monitored quantum systems** (García-Pintos, Liu and Gorshkov 2026) a control Hamiltonian can replicate the stochastic trajectories of a continuously measured system, and feedback built on it can blur, amplify or overcompensate the measurement disturbance, producing dynamics within the controlled subsystem more consistent with time running backward than forward. The scoped observation is operational: at the level of description the arrow's direction is agent-relative and locally reversible, robust in practice; nothing follows for fundamental time. A third analogue was added on 15 October 2026 (§22.10): in an isolated **cold-atom system** with a time-independent Hamiltonian, partitioned optically into a "dark" and a "bright" sector whose effective Hamiltonian is minisuperspace-like, the natural internal clock field is *not* monotonic across the sector's recollapse, whereas an operationally defined *entropic time* — built from the coarse-grained entropy exchanged between the sectors — orders the data monotonically, yields an effective Schrödinger equation that reproduces the measurements, and stops at the sector's "heat death" (Barontini et al., Phys. Rev. Research 8 (2026) L022047). The scoped observation is the ledger's own division (§9.4): an internal clock supplies order and fails where it is non-monotonic; the arrow in the analogue is read off a coarse-grained entropy of a particular state, which is Proposition 11.1's pattern, not a derivation from structure. All three are analogues of the plan's structures, not instances of them.

**17.4 Decoherence and records** (unchanged). Decoherence is time-symmetric; it produces records only in the presence of (a). "Becoming" — whether conditional correlations constitute a real flow — remains excluded from the falsifiable core (§9, open item 5), with causal-set sequential growth as the one programme that offers a mechanism for it.

**In plain terms.** Why does time have a direction? Two questions hide in that one: why the universe started tidy, and why there is one direction rather than two pointing away from a middle. On the first, the Lighthouse bets that the tidy start was law, not luck, and now knows where to look: the one distinguished state the new algebra offers is the maximally *untidy* one, so the tidy one must be singled out by something else — ideally by the same measure that gives the flow. On the second, physics has no answer and this plan offers none; what revelation says about the direction of the world stays in its own register.
