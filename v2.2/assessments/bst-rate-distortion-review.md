# Clue 8 — *The Rate–Distortion Theory of Bounded Sequential Transduction* (Abaee, Zenodo 2026): evaluation, verification, steelman, completion

**Source.** A. Abaee, "The Rate–Distortion Theory of Bounded Sequential Transduction: A Comparative Syntax for Finite-State Approximation", Zenodo preprint v1, published 2 September 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22260638. Files (md5 checked against the record): `automata_corrected_v9.tex` (18,328 lines, ≈89,700 words; 55 theorems, 38 propositions, 21 lemmas, 32 corollaries, 61 definitions), `highlights.txt`, `supplementary.tar.gz` (three Python programs, machine tables, logs, a Lean 4 project), `aak_multiletter_proof_check.docx` (a 3,000-word proof-check report on one theorem). Same author as the Rev. 2.8 opfibration manuscript (clue 5); the new paper does not cite it and shares no theorem with it. Local copy: `uploads/zenodo-22260638/`; pointer file `clues/clue-08-bst-rate-distortion.md`.

**One-sentence description.** A pure-mathematics/theoretical-CS monograph: given a sequential object (a deterministic transducer, a controlled stochastic process, or a channel), what is the best approximation by a machine with at most *M* internal states, measured by a regime-specific loss — and how do the three regimes (commitment / retention / grounding) compare? The "rate" is the state budget *M* (a right-congruence index or an operator rank), not bits; the "distortion" is Boolean mismatch, stationary KL, or operator norm.

**Standing instruction applied:** evaluate, verify, steelman, complete — proportionate to the object. The object is 90,000 words; the verification below is spot-checking of the headline results and full reproduction of the shipped computational package, not a line-by-line audit.

**Overall grade of the paper:** [C] for its exact layer (which is correct and, in the majority of items, classical); [H] for its conditional and complexity layers; the schema itself is a vocabulary rather than a theorem. Its most valuable property is procedural: every principal result carries an explicit status label, the open problems are stated with success criteria, the computational claims ship with code that reproduces exactly, and the one high-exposure theorem without external support (multiletter AAK) is flagged as conditional and matched to the literature's open problem. By the standards of this project's own §22 (verification status by source), it is the best-behaved document the user has sent.

**Weaknesses, stated plainly.** (i) Density: ≈90,000 words for a handful of non-textbook theorems (items 3, 8–11 and the lower bounds in 10); many "Exact" results are identities (1, 2, 5, 6-lower-bound). (ii) The Lean artefact covers arithmetic lemmas only; a reader who sees "Lean 4, 0 sorry" should not infer that any theorem of the paper is formalized. (iii) The multiletter AAK theorem should be called a transport lemma, not a theorem, since its content lives in its hypothesis. (iv) The AI-assisted drafting (declared in the TeX) shows: status-label repetition, footnotes disambiguating the phrase "grounding gap" from unrelated NLP usage, "Turn 24/26" residues in the Lean README. None of this affects correctness. (v) The "rate–distortion" framing invites a comparison with Shannon R(D) that the paper does not and cannot cash out (no coding theorem); the honest name is "finite-state approximation theory with typed budgets".

## 3. Steelman — what is genuinely there

1. **A disciplined comparative syntax.** Three literatures (Myhill–Nerode minimization; computational mechanics / ε-machines; Hankel-operator approximate minimization of weighted automata) are placed on one chassis — histories, finite-index right congruences, variational gaps — with the explicit theorem that the chassis does *not* collapse them (independence theorem). This is organizational work of real use to anyone who confuses "number of states" across the three senses.
2. **Controlled unifilar retention** extends the causal-state programme from processes to input-driven transductions, with the lumpability constraint made precise (support-relative right congruence) and the zero threshold correctly located at the refinement index rather than the kernel count. That the threshold is *not* the number of distinct kernels is the kind of point that is routinely gotten wrong.
3. **Sharp constants, correct caveats.** Constant 1 in probability coordinates (sharp); the Fisher/natural-parameter chart only in the interior; Hankel equality only under embedding; Ambainis not Freivalds. The paper refuses several easy overstatements.
4. **Lower bounds on the temporal axis.** The persistent-stream Θ(M log M) lower bound via an explicit adversary on a single never-reset stream, and the unconditional active bound through halving over H_M × Q, are the chapter's real content; the enumerations give exact small-M depths matching the stated claims.
5. **Reproducibility.** Two programs, exact logs, a reproduction table that itself lists what was *not* re-run. This is unusual and should be acknowledged.

## 4. Relation to the problem of time and to this plan

This is the step that matters for the thread. The paper is relevant **indirectly**, in four precise ways, and **not at all** in the way a title containing "sequential" and "temporal axis" might suggest.

**(a) Time is an input, not an output — and the paper says so by construction.** Every object is defined over prefix-ordered histories; a transducer is a *causal* function I^ℕ → O^ℕ; the "temporal axis" is the round index of an online protocol. Nothing in the paper derives an order, an orientation, or a duration; it presupposes all three. This is not a flaw — it is a theory of bounded modelling *within* a given order — but it means clue 8 cannot be a source for Part III of the plan. It is, however, a fourth independent instance of Prop. 11.1 in its weak form: where a formalism contains a time-direction, that direction entered as a hypothesis (here: the prefix order on histories and the causal-state convention past → future). Recorded as such in §10.6 (item 73).

**(b) It corrects the "epistemic asymptote" and the v2.0 rate–distortion shell from the right side.** v2.0 §5.4 wanted a rate–distortion price on clocks and an "intercept principle" as a wall against intrinsic observers; Rev. 2.8 withdrew the asymptote and v2.2 §10.6 demoted the shell to "a formal shell, decoupled". Clue 8 is the author's rigorous version of the underlying intuition, and it shows what the intuition legitimately amounts to: a **gap curve** Δ(M) with **zero thresholds at finite budget** — κ_det(F) (commitment), N* (retention), rank H_ν (linear grounding) — and a genuine asymptote only in the infinite-rank case, where Δ_unres(M) = σ_{M+1} → 0 without reaching 0. So "epistemic asymptote" was wrong in both directions: for finite-state targets the bounded observer is exact above a finite threshold; for infinite-rank targets the shortfall is σ_{M+1}, a computable number, not a wall. Item 17 stays withdrawn; §10.6 gains the correct formal object (item 74).

**(c) It supplies the formal template the informational layer lacked — as vocabulary, not derivation.** §4.2 ("finite information density", "interface structure") and N-1 ("predictive processing, rigorously bounded") name a bounded observer without a mathematics for it. Clue 8 gives one: an observer with budget M facing a sequential world incurs (i) a representational cost Δ(M) with the three thresholds above, (ii) an identification cost Θ(M log M) mistakes before its model is right (passive, streaming or active), and (iii) a direction-dependence of (i) — see (d). These are theorems about *machines*, and under this plan's own rule (§4.2: "the anchors are vocabulary, not derivations"; §6 "borrowed fit") they confirm nothing about the world; but they give N-1 and §4.2 a precise register in which claims can be stated and falsified. Item 75; N-1 row annotated.

**(d) Completion: the one place where the apparatus touches the arrow.** The paper's retention regime is the causal-state construction of computational mechanics, and that construction has a known time-asymmetry theorem the paper does not use: for a stationary process, the forward ε-machine (past → future) and the reverse ε-machine (future → past) need not be isomorphic, and their statistical complexities differ — *causal irreversibility* Ξ = C_μ⁺ − C_μ⁻ ≠ 0 — even though the entropy rate is the same in both directions (Crutchfield–Ellison–Mahoney, PRL 103, 094101 (2009)). Translated into the paper's own terms: the retention gap curve is direction-dependent, Δ_ret⁺(M) ≠ Δ_ret⁻(M), and the zero thresholds N*⁺, N*⁻ can differ. Two consequences, both consistent with this plan and neither in the paper:

- *What it is:* a sharp **diagnostic** of time-asymmetry in a law, visible to a bounded predictor as a budget difference. A process whose law is time-reversal invariant has isomorphic forward and reverse machines; a budget asymmetry therefore certifies that the law is not time-symmetric. This is exactly the shape Prop. 11.1 requires: the arrow is read off a contingent asymmetry in the object, never manufactured by the observer's formalism.
- *What it is not:* a source of the arrow. The asymmetry is in the process; the bounded observer only pays for it. Anyone proposing "the arrow of time is the asymmetry of bounded prediction" has the direction of explanation reversed, and the plan's rule 11 (imagery orients, nothing enters Part I) applies.

Stated as an open problem in the paper's own format (success criterion included) and logged as item 76: *Direction-dependent retention.* Hypotheses: stationary ergodic output process with finite forward and reverse causal-state sets. Statement sought: a formula or bound for Δ_ret⁺(M) − Δ_ret⁻(M) in terms of Ξ and the crypticity χ, and a characterization of the processes with Δ_ret⁺ ≡ Δ_ret⁻ (conjecturally: exactly the time-reversal-invariant laws, up to isomorphism of the two machines). Success criterion: either the characterization or a counterexample — a non-reversible law with identical forward and reverse gap curves at every M.

**(e) What is *not* imported.** No identification of the state budget with Bekenstein/holographic bounds (D-11 is an interface inequality, not a count of Mealy states); no reading of "retention" as memory in the phenomenological sense of §17; no reading of the online protocol as physical time. The Shannon shell of §10.6 (R(D) in bits for clock readings) and the state-budget theory of clue 8 are different objects, related by analogy only; §10.6 now says so.
