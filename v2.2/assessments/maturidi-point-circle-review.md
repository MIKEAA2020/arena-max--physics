# Review: the point–circle derivation of the Māturīdī position (clue #6a)

Source: `clues/clue-06a-maturidi.md`. Formal checks: `v2.2/verify/q_kernel.py`, section 9 (9a–9e; 34/34 overall).

## The argument as given
1. Being radiates from the Point (God) to the circle (creation); every radius exists only by participation.
2. There is no determination without being.
3. A radius can determine (orient, select) but cannot give being.
4. Hence the *being* of a human act is created by God alone; the human contributes the determination (choice).
5. This is the Māturīdī khalq/ikhtiyār scheme; therefore the GPP structure *entails* Māturīdism.

## Verdict: not an entailment; a model of layered concurrence at best ([P])

**1. Category.** The point–circle picture is admitted in the Lighthouse as orientation only (§4, [P]) and is explicitly forbidden as a derivation engine (§6, what to avoid). Nothing graded [P] can entail a doctrine; at most it can illustrate one.

**2. Equivocation on "give being".** Two claims are run together: (i) no creature is the *ultimate* source of being (ījād ab initio) — common ground of Ashʿarī, Māturīdī, Imāmī, Thomist and Taymiyyan accounts alike, and in the kernel Q1 + Q9; (ii) no creature is the *proximate* producer of an effect's existence through a power given and conserved by God — the thesis that actually separates Māturīdī from the Imāmī ṭūlī reading and from Thomist concurrence. Step 3 is true under (i) and question-begging under (ii). The picture contains no inter-radius causation at all, so it has no resources to decide (ii).

**3. Proves too much.** Nothing in steps 1–3 mentions free acts. If the argument works, it works for every effect: fire does not produce burning, seed does not produce plant. That is occasionalism about nature (P*), which is (a) stronger than the piece's conclusion — Māturīdism about acts does not need it (check 9b) — and (b) not forced by the text or the kernel: kernel + verses are satisfiable with P* (9d) *and* with its negation (9e). The piece therefore cannot claim entailment even for its own weaker conclusion.

**4. Internal tension with the tradition it defends.** The later Māturīdī solution to the regress problem — the *juzʾī ikhtiyār* of Ṣadr al-Sharīʿa, an iʿtibārī "neither existent nor non-existent" — is precisely a determination that is not a being, which step 2 forbids. Either step 2 goes or the standard Māturīdī device goes.

**5. Text.** The Qur'an applies the verb *khalaqa* to a creature acting by leave: 3:49 / 5:110 (*annī akhluqu lakum … bi-idhni llāh*), 23:14 (*aḥsan al-khāliqīn*, a superlative presupposing other "creators" in a lesser sense). 37:96 is ambiguous and asserts creation (Q1) under either reading (checks 8d). The text does not exclude creaturely production; it excludes creaturely *independence* (Q9, Q11).

## Steelman
Replace "a radius cannot give being" with "being flows *through* the radius": then the picture says that every creaturely act exists by God's continuous giving and is at the same time genuinely the creature's — layered concurrence with real distinction, which is the kernel's verdict (§4.1) and the common core of both schools. Read this way the piece is a good *illustration* of Q1 + Q2 + Q3 + Q9; it simply does not reach the mechanism question, which the text leaves open (checks 8a–8d, 9c–9e).
