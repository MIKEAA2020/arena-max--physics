# Clue 12 — Ciupa, *Is the Thermodynamic Arrow of Time an Illusion? A Bayesian Brain Perspective* (LinkedIn, 8 Dec 2024; 1,474 words)

**Asked (15 October 2026):** "any clues here, anything worth borrowing from, either as is or after modifications?" Text archived: `search/clue11/thermodynamic-arrow-time-illusion-bayesian-brain-martin-ciupa-lwyze.txt`.

**What it says.** (1) The second law gives a macroscopic arrow, at odds with time-symmetric microphysics. (2) The brain anchors the past by memory and projects the future by Bayesian prediction; memory and prediction create the experience of flow. (3) Under the Bayesian-brain hypothesis (Friston 2010; Hohwy 2013) memories are priors, the brain "implicitly assumes an increase in entropy" when predicting, and the timeline is a construct. (4) Hence the thermodynamic arrow "might reflect our brain's biased interpretation of entropy": the laws are symmetric; the brain's entropy-reliance is an evolutionary adaptation "not evidence of time's intrinsic directionality"; cognitive smoothing; "no encoding mechanism for future events". (5) Time may be emergent, relational (Rovelli), and its directionality "a cognitive artefact". The bibliography (Boltzmann, Price 1996, Friston 2010, Hohwy 2013, Eagleman 2008, Barbour 1999, Rovelli 2017, Reichenbach 1956) is real — unlike the author's zero article (clue 11).

**Short answer.** Nothing to borrow as stated: the title's thesis is false, and the article contradicts it itself (§2: "the thermodynamic arrow describes an objective feature of the physical world"; §6: "entropy drives the macroscopic arrow"). It runs the explanation backwards — the brain's temporal asymmetry is a *consequence* of the thermodynamic arrow, not a lens that might make it illusory. Turned the right way round, it yields one thing the plan should have stated and had not: the derivative status of the psychological arrow, at theorem grade, with references. That is added (§17.1(a); N-1 scope note). The article's defensible residue — that the experienced *flow* is a feature of how observers process information — is already in the plan (§9.3 item 5; §9.4 row 5) and in the literature it cites.

## 2. Why (i) is backwards — the result that should be in the plan

- **Records need the arrow.** A trace of the past exists only because the universe was in a lower-entropy state earlier (Reichenbach 1956, the branch-structure account; Albert 2000, *Time and Chance*). Rovelli (arXiv:2003.06687; *Entropy* 24 (2022) 1022) gives sufficient thermodynamic conditions for macroscopic traces — system separation, temperature differences, long thermalization times — and shows the mechanism "transforms low entropy into available information". So "memories as priors" presupposes the gradient.
- **A memory's arrow must align with the thermodynamic arrow — generically, even if the memory is reversible.** Mlodinow and Brun (PRE 89 (2014) 052102; arXiv:1310.1095) derive the alignment from two principles — the robustness of the thermodynamic arrow to small perturbations of the state, and the requirement that a memory not be fine-tuned to the system it records — for any physical system that preserves a record, dissipative or not. The "no encoding mechanism for future events" that the article offers as evidence of a cognitive bias is this theorem seen from inside.
- **Past, present and future as IGUS notions.** Hartle (Am. J. Phys. 73 (2005) 101; gr-qc/0403001): the division into past, present and future is not a property of four-dimensional spacetime but a description of how information-gathering-and-utilizing systems process information, consistent with the four-dimensional laws, and with their origin in how such systems evolved. This is the correct form of the article's claim (ii) — and Hartle's IGUS has its arrow fixed by the record-forming requirement above, so it cannot be turned into (i).
- **The "evolutionary adaptation" argument is self-undermining.** If brains evolved "in a universe governed by thermodynamics", the asymmetry they track is in the universe; an adaptation to an environmental gradient is evidence *for* the gradient. The illusion reading would need the brain's asymmetry to exist without an environmental one, which contradicts the premise.
- **"Macroscopic/statistical, therefore possibly illusory" is a non sequitur.** Temperature is statistical and not an illusion. The plan grades the arrow *contingent* (a fact about the state, Prop. 11.1; A9; D-8), which is a different thing from *unreal*.
- **The Bayesian-brain detail is misdescribed.** Predictive processing minimizes variational free energy — the surprisal of sensory states — which is the organism keeping *itself* in a small set of low-entropy states (Friston's "resisting the second law"); it does not "assume entropy increase" about the world. The one place entropy enters correctly is as the precondition for the priors (records) the inference uses.

**Family resemblance.** This is the same error, in a cognitive register, as clue 9's D4 ("ε-machine update = arrow", graded [F]) and clue 11's TES ("anchors drive the arrow"): an observer-level asymmetry projected as the source of the arrow. The ledger (§9.4) already says where observer-level time stands — relative to a meta-time that is itself what is to be accounted for.

## 4. On the Einstein epigraph

"The distinction between past, present and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion" is from Einstein's letter to the Besso family (21 March 1955), written as consolation after Michele Besso's death. It is a remark about *passage* in a four-dimensional world — claim (ii) — not about the entropy gradient; it does not support the title.
